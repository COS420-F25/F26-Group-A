### Problem Statement:
Students often struggle to learn how to improve their learning and performance while also lacking a supportive community to help them reflect on their experiences. Reflection groups can provide this support, but organizing productive groups can be difficult and time-consuming for facilitators and students alike. 

It is also hard to get enough motivation to try a new practice like reflection groups, for students and even instructors. People have ideas about why reflection groups would not work, based on prior knowledge/experiences or misconceptions about what reflection groups are. 

For example, students may be reluctant to seek out reflection groups on their own. At the same time, instructors and other facilitators may struggle to find and match students into groups that work well together. 

Reflection groups can be more productive when members share similar experiences, challenges, and perspectives because these similarities can make it easier to empathize, exchange relevant advice, be more vulnerable, and feel comfortable sharing struggles. However, finding these compatible group members can require significant effort from facilitators. 

How might we design supportive tools to enable students and instructors to get over the motivation barrier to trying reflection groups, then find and sustain compatible reflection groups, so students can have more beneficial reflection experiences, while requiring less facilitation effort (even when an instructor does not organize groups)?
